Host a month

A different educator sets each month's challenge, which keeps the problems grounded in real classrooms rather than drifting toward whatever is technically interesting. This year's hosts include classroom educators, podcast hosts, and education consultants from around the world, each bringing a challenge from their own corner of education.

What hosting asks of you

Four things — two to three hours in total across the month.

  • A half-page challenge brief. Describe a real need from your practice and where it comes from. You do not need to know how to solve it. It is better if you do not.
  • A short video introduction. Two to three minutes, phone camera is fine, introducing yourself and your challenge in your own words. It goes on the site with your month.
  • Answering builder questions, once a week at most. Builders ask questions through the site, and I collect them for you. Expect one short message from me each week with the few questions worth answering, often none at all. Your answers are posted with the challenge so every builder benefits. Builders never have your email.
  • Co-hosting the closing share-out. Sixty minutes on Google Meet in the final week, recorded and shared on our YouTube channel. You frame the need, the builders do the talking.

What a brief looks like

The need: "Cold calling is supposed to spread participation around the room, but the same hands and the same names come up again and again, and keeping it fair from memory adds one more thing to track while teaching."

The challenge: "Build your own random name picker. Any AI assistant, any free host, any twist that fits your classroom."

That is the whole shape: a real need, honestly described, with the solving left entirely to the builders.

Ask about hosting a month

Tell us a little about yourself and the challenge you might bring, and we will get back to you about open months.
